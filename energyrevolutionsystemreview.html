Energy Revolution System Review: How It Works, What You Get & What to Consider

This review is based on the seller's own published sales materials, checkout page, product cover art, and independent research into the underlying science. We have not independently lab-tested a completed build.

Review overview

Energy Revolution System is a low-cost digital guide teaching a Tesla-inspired, coil-based DIY electricity project. It is a legitimate ClickBank product with a real refund window; its marketing savings claims are unverified and should be treated with caution.

Format

Digital download (PDF + bonus e-books)

Price

Seller checkout has shown ~$39, listed against $97–$149

Guarantee

60 days, via ClickBank

What is it?

Energy Revolution System is marketed as a beginner-friendly guide for building a small, supplemental electricity device based on the winding concept behind Tesla's bifilar coil. It is sold and delivered entirely online: checkout is processed through ClickBank, and access to the guide is granted immediately, with no physical shipment involved.

What you actually get

The purchase includes the core guide (build steps, diagrams, and a materials list) plus several bonus e-books shown on the product's own bundle artwork — reference material on home-energy basics, battery storage, and alternative power sources. Everything is accessed through a members' dashboard, viewable on a phone, tablet, or computer.

How it is supposed to work

Per the seller's description: you source common electrical parts, wind a coil using the bifilar (two-parallel-wire) pattern shown in the guide, wire it into a small circuit, test it, and use the finished unit to power small loads such as lights or a phone charger. This is the seller's account of the process rather than an independently engineered specification, and no third-party lab report on the exact build's output has been published.

Science behind the claims

The reference points are real: electromagnetic induction is foundational physics, and Tesla's bifilar coil is documented in U.S. Patent 512,340 (1894). Bifilar winding is still studied today, including in peer-reviewed IEEE research on transformer design. What is not independently established is whether a home-built version of this concept can deliver the specific bill-reduction percentages used in marketing. Any device remains subject to the law of conservation of energy: it cannot output more usable electricity than the energy put into building and running it. See the full science discussion and Tesla/bifilar coil explainer on our homepage for sourced detail.

We cannot verify specific dollar figures for this exact guide, but the general pattern — modest supplemental output rather than transformative savings — is the realistic frame to bring into a purchase decision.
